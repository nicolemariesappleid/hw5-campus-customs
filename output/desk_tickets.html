Who Campus Customs is (and why it matters for these tickets)

Campus Customs is a family-run shop at 57 Broadway, across from Yale and two doors down from the Yale Bookstore (Barnes & Noble, 77 Broadway). It began as a Yale memorabilia store in the mid-1970s, draws a lot of undergraduates because of its Broadway spot, and has grown into a screen-printing, embroidery, graphic-design and web-store business, with stores in Branford, CT and Celebration, FL. It has run its own Yale-community promotions, such as a 40%-off early-morning sale and 20% off for Yale employees. Our database is a tiny slice of that business: 4 SKUs, 3 vendors, 1 lease (the "Chapel Street shop"), and one checking account holding $3,400.00 on desk date 2026-08-31. That date is the start of fall term, when first-years and families are walking Broadway, which is why a size-S tee being out of stock (ticket 101) and a student club ordering 20 hoodies (ticket 103) are real stakes, not toy ones.

1. How I would evaluate the agents on each ticket

I grade each run on four things, using facts I can check against the database: (a) right facts (did the agents quote real rows rather than the ticket's wording), (b) right people (did the Boss call only the specialists the ticket needs), (c) safe money (are drafts correct and is nothing paid twice), and (d) cost (model calls and tokens for the work done).

Ticket 101 — Tauhid Zaman, 1 × Classic Bulldog Tee (CC-TEE-WHITE) size S

  • What a correct run has to find. check_inventory returns qty 0 for S in Aisle B (M has 5, L 3, XL 2), so the shortfall is 1. The ticket links invoice 501: Bulldog Print Co, $840.00, "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S", due 2026-08-28, so it is 3 days overdue on the desk date. A strong run connects the two: the reprint Tauhid is waiting for is the very job the shop hasn't paid for.
  • What earns full marks. Accounting drafts the $840 invoice payment (prepare_payment, which also confirms it isn't already paid). Inventory does not draft a second reprint PO, which would double-order from the same vendor. Customer Service drafts a note to Tauhid that is honest: no S on the shelf today, size M is in stock if he wants it, and no promised date beyond Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead time.
  • What loses marks. Promising "ships tomorrow", a duplicate PO, or a customer note that implies anything was sent. Our guardrails keep messages as drafts, but they can't catch a wrong promise inside a draft. Only the human reading it can.
  • re-check Grade against the 101 tab's Actual section: was invoice 501 linked to the shortfall, and how many tokens did it take?

Ticket 102 — Elm City Properties, "Rent due"

  • What a correct run has to find. get_lease(1) returns $2,400.00 monthly rent for the Chapel Street shop, next due 2026-09-02, which is exactly "2 days" after the desk date, so the email checks out. find_payments finds no rent payment yet, and checking ($3,400) covers it.
  • What earns full marks. One rent draft for exactly $2,400 that would leave $1,000, with the Boss marking it as needing your approval.
  • The cross-ticket test most runs will fail. Each ticket runs alone. The 102 run has no tool that lists all open bills (only get_invoice by id), so it can't see that invoice 501's $840 is also due. Paying both leaves $160. A great run would say so; our tools make that nearly impossible, and that's a tooling gap, not an agent failure.

Ticket 103 — Yale AI Club, 20 × CC-HOOD-NAVY size M, bulk discount

  • What a correct run has to find. Only 8 size-M hoodies are on hand, so the shortfall is 12. (The shop holds 32 across all sizes: S 4, M 8, L 14, XL 6, but the club asked for M.) Pricing is $58.00 list against $22.00 cost. check_margin at 15% off gives $49.30, a $27.30 margin per hoodie and $546.00 profit on 20. The price stays above cost all the way down to a 62% discount.
  • What earns full marks. A specific discount backed by margin numbers, plus a restock PO for 12 from Bulldog Print Co (apparel, 5-day lead) costing 12 × $22 = $264.00.
  • Where the run gets stopped (shown in the dry run, and the numbers alone guarantee it). After 101 and 102 are approved, checking holds $160, so the $264 PO is refused at approval with "Not enough cash: 264.00 needed, 160.00 in checking." The agents can't know this ahead of time because they don't see the other tickets' pending drafts.
  • What our app doesn't enforce. The discount itself is never recorded or approved. Only payments and POs go through the approval queue, so a run that "offers 25% off" leaves no trace in the database.

The finding that ties all three together

The three drafts add up to $840 + $2,400 + $264 = $3,504, which is $104 more than the $3,400 in checking. So one bill always gets refused, and the order the tickets run in silently decides which one:

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Honestly, at this size all three tickets would be simpler for one agent holding the 13 MCP tools, with the same approval queue.

  • 102 is a straight line: get_ticket → get_lease → find_payments → prepare_payment. With the team, every agent needs at least two model calls (one to use a tool, one to report), so Boss → Facilities → Accounting costs at least 8 model calls, which is exactly what the dry run logged. One agent needs about 4, and each call resends the instructions and tool list, so tokens roughly halve too.
  • Hand-offs lose context. A helper only gets the short request string, not the conversation. On 101, Accounting gets "invoice 501 is overdue" but not "a customer is waiting on that reprint", which is the fact that makes paying it urgent. One agent keeps everything in one context.
  • The safety doesn't come from the team. The real protections are in the tools and the human-only approve route: payments are drafts, re-checked at approval, never paid twice, and refused when cash is short. Splitting into five agents adds no protection there. Least privilege (Customer Service can't draft payments) is nice, but one agent with a draft-only toolset is just as safe.
  • Where the team would pay off: with Campus Customs' full business (decoration jobs, a web store, three stores) the tool count and domain knowledge would outgrow one prompt. That's when a Production or Fulfillment specialist with its own tools earns its cost.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with today's tools

  1. The Crest Mug is out during family visits. CC-MUG-CREST shows 0 on hand in Aisle D. A ticket like "parents asking for Crest Mugs" goes Inventory (check_inventory → 0, list_vendors → Elm City Gifts, "mugs and small goods", 3-day lead) → Accounting (prepare_purchase_order(2, CC-MUG-CREST, OS, 24) = 24 × $3.50 = $84.00). That fits even in the $160 left after the board-order run, and Customer Service drafts a "back in about 3 days" note.
  2. A Yale-employee or early-bird promotion. Campus Customs has run 20%-off-for-Yale-employees and 40%-off early-morning sales. Accounting can price them with check_margin before the doors open: the Bulldog Tee at 20% off is $22.40 against $8 cost ($14.40 margin), and the Yale Cap (40 on hand) at 40% off is $13.20 against $6 cost ($7.20 margin). The Boss can approve the promotion with margins in hand, all from the pricing table.
  3. A size-run question from a team or club. "Do you have hoodies for 4 smalls, 8 mediums, 6 XLs?" Inventory checks each size of CC-HOOD-NAVY (S 4, M 8, XL 6, which is exactly enough, and L 14 spare). Accounting quotes the group price with check_margin, and Customer Service drafts the reply. No money moves, so nothing waits for approval.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

  1. A custom-decorated club order. Ticket 103's club will probably want its own logo printed on the hoodies, which is Campus Customs' core work (screen printing, embroidery, design, virtual samples). Our database has no artwork, no decoration costs, no production capacity and no job status. Needed: a Production agent with tools like quote_decoration(method, colors, qty), check_press_schedule(date) and create_job; a Design/Proof agent that handles artwork uploads and sample approvals; and a Yale marks check for any design using Yale names or logos, against the university's trademark and licensing rules. That last one is a human sign-off, not something to automate.
  2. The cash squeeze across tickets. As shown above, $3,504 of bills meet $3,400 of cash, and ticket order decides who gets paid. The team can't see all pending obligations, has no income data (no sales or receivables tables), and can't take a deposit from the Yale AI Club before ordering. Needed: list_open_obligations(), cash_forecast(days) and record_customer_deposit() tools; a Controller agent that ranks bills (rent and overdue vendors before new stock); and a Boss that runs across all open tickets, not one at a time. All still drafts that need approval.
  3. Web orders and the other stores. Campus Customs sells online worldwide and runs stores in Branford and Celebration, FL, but our inventory table has one location field ("Aisle A"), no store column, no orders, and no write tools at all, so stock can't even go down when Tauhid finally buys his tee. Customer Service can draft but never send. Needed: an orders table and Fulfillment agent with reserve_stock, transfer_stock(from_store, to_store) and create_shipment; a multi-store inventory schema; and an approve-then-send outbox for customer emails, with a human clicking Send just like Approve works for money.
