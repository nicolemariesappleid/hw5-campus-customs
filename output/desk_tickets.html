Who Campus Customs is (and why it matters for these tickets)

Campus Customs is a family-run shop at 57 Broadway, across from Yale and two doors down from the Yale Bookstore (Barnes & Noble, 77 Broadway). It began as a Yale memorabilia store in the mid-1970s, draws a lot of undergraduates because of its Broadway spot, and has grown into a screen-printing, embroidery, graphic-design and web-store business, with stores in Branford, CT and Celebration, FL. It has run its own Yale-community promotions, such as a 40%-off early-morning sale and 20% off for Yale employees. Our database is a tiny slice of that business: 4 SKUs, 3 vendors, 1 lease (the "Chapel Street shop"), and one checking account holding $3,400.00 on desk date 2026-08-31. That date is the start of fall term, when first-years and families are walking Broadway, which is why a size-S tee being out of stock (101) and a student club ordering 20 hoodies (103) are real stakes.

Ticket 101 — Tauhid Zaman, 1 × Classic Bulldog Tee (CC-TEE-WHITE) size S · Boss's call: blocked

  • What went right. Inventory checked the exact SKU and size (check_inventory → 0 on hand, shortfall 1), pulled list_vendors and get_shop_date, and correctly worked out the earliest restock as 2026-09-05 (Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead from 08-31).
  • The hand-off failure. Inventory knew the vendor was Bulldog Print Co (id 1), but its request to Accounting only said "Does restocking require a cost or PO?", without the vendor. Accounting was two levels down, so it couldn't ask back, and it called prepare_purchase_order(vendor_id=0, …). The tool refused: "No vendor 0 in the database." The Boss then marked the ticket blocked because "a valid vendor ID is needed". The facts were all in the system; they were lost between two agents.
  • The bigger miss: invoice 501 was ignored. get_ticket returned invoice_id: 501, the $840 rush reprint of this exact tee and size, 3 days overdue. No agent called get_invoice. So the team tried to order a second reprint of a tee that's already being reprinted, which would have double-ordered from Bulldog Print Co if the vendor id had been right. That's the exact mistake I listed as "loses marks" before the run.
  • Nobody answered the customer. Customer Service was never called, so Tauhid gets no draft telling him size S is out and size M (5 on hand) is available.
  • Grade D: the stock check was correct and it failed safely (no money, no wrong message), but it missed the linked invoice and the customer, and lost the vendor in a hand-off.

  • Grade A−.

Ticket 103 — Yale AI Club, 20 × CC-HOOD-NAVY size M, bulk discount · Boss's call: needs human approval

  • The Boss skipped Inventory. It sent the whole ticket to Accounting only. So no one learned that just 8 size-M hoodies are on hand. The Boss's final call says to "keep the 20 hoodies at list price ($58 each, $1,160 total)", which reads as if the shop can fill 20 today. It can't: it's 12 short.
  • Accounting was correct but timid. It ran check_margin only at list price ($58 → $720 profit, 62.1% margin) and recommended no discount "absent further authorization". It never tested a single discounted price, even though the tool would have shown that 15% off ($49.30) still earns $546. For a student club asking for a bulk deal at the start of term, "no discount, no plan" is a weak answer.
  • No restock plan, no club reply. No vendor lookup, no PO for the 12 missing, and no Customer Service draft to the club. It was the cheapest run (6 calls), because it did the least.
  • Grade C−: safe and arithmetically right, but it gave a misleading fulfillment answer and ignored half the ticket (stock).

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

The real run makes this case more strongly than I could have predicted.

  • 102 cost 9 model calls and 7,905 tokens for a four-tool job (get_ticket → get_lease → find_payments → prepare_payment). One agent would need about 4 calls, and each call resends the instructions and tool list, so roughly half the tokens. The team also repeated find_payments and get_shop_date, because Accounting didn't know Facilities had already checked them.
  • 101 failed because of a hand-off. One agent that had just called list_vendors would still have Bulldog Print Co's id 1 in front of it when drafting the PO. It would also still have invoice_id: 501 from get_ticket in view, making it far more likely to notice the reprint was already ordered.
  • 103 failed because of routing. One agent with both check_inventory and check_margin doesn't have to decide who to ask; it just checks stock and margin.
  • The team added no safety. Every protection that mattered was in the tools or the human-only approve route: the PO with vendor 0 was refused by the tool, and the rent was paid only after a human click. One agent with draft-only tools would be just as safe.
  • When the team would earn its cost: with Campus Customs' whole business (decoration jobs, a web store, three stores), a single prompt and tool list would get too big. A small team with better hand-offs, where the Boss passes the full ticket and specialists must pass ids, would make sense then.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with today's tools

  1. Paying the overdue reprint (follow-up to 101). Invoice 501, $840 to Bulldog Print Co, is still open and 3 days late. A ticket that names it directly goes to Accounting: get_invoice(501) → find_payments(invoice, 501) → prepare_payment(invoice, 501, 840). That leaves $160 of today's $1,000. Customer Service then drafts Tauhid's note: S arrives about 2026-09-05, or he can take M today.
  2. The Crest Mug is out during family visits. CC-MUG-CREST shows 0 in Aisle D. Inventory uses list_vendors and gets Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead). Accounting runs prepare_purchase_order(2, CC-MUG-CREST, OS, 24): 24 × $3.50 = $84.00, which easily fits the remaining $1,000. This works if the request passes vendor id 2, the lesson from 101.
  3. A Yale-employee or early-bird promotion. Campus Customs has run 20%-off-for-Yale-employees and 40%-off early-morning sales. Accounting can price them with check_margin: the Bulldog Tee at 20% off is $22.40 against $8 cost ($14.40 margin), and the Yale Cap (40 on hand) at 40% off is $13.20 against $6 cost ($7.20 margin). That's the margin test 103 never ran on a real discounted price.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

  1. A custom-decorated club order. The Yale AI Club in 103 will likely want its logo on the hoodies, which is Campus Customs' core screen-printing and embroidery work. Our database has no artwork, decoration costs, press capacity or job status. Needed: a Production agent with quote_decoration(method, colors, qty), check_press_schedule(date) and create_job; a Design/Proof agent for artwork uploads and virtual-sample sign-off; and a human check of any Yale names or marks against the university's trademark and licensing rules.
  2. Deciding which bills to pay when cash is short. After rent, $1,000 remains. Invoice 501 ($840) and a hoodie restock ($264) together need $1,104, and the team can't even see both at once: there's no tool listing all open bills, no sales income, and no way to take a deposit from the club before ordering. Needed: list_open_obligations(), cash_forecast(days) and record_customer_deposit() tools, plus a Controller agent that ranks bills (rent and overdue vendors before new stock) across all tickets at once, still draft-only and still human-approved.
  3. Web orders and the other stores. Campus Customs sells online and runs stores in Branford and Celebration, FL. Our inventory table has one location field ("Aisle A"), no store column, no orders and no write tools, so stock never goes down when something sells, and 103's 8 hoodies can't be topped up from Branford. Customer Service can only draft. Needed: an orders table and a Fulfillment agent with reserve_stock, transfer_stock(from_store, to_store) and create_shipment; a multi-store inventory schema; and an approve-then-send outbox for customer emails.
